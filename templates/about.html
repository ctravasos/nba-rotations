How it works

Timeouts and breaks

Every NBA quarter has two mandatory TV timeouts. If neither team has called a timeout by 6:59, the officials take one at the next dead ball; same again at 2:59 for the second. A team-called timeout before those marks counts as the mandatory one, so the TV breaks often come earlier than 7:00 / 3:00 — especially after 1Q.

That makes TO1 and TO2 simply the first and second timeouts of each quarter, whenever they happen. The site tracks 1Q–3Q TO1/TO2 and 4Q TO1, plus the 1Q/2Q and 3Q/4Q breaks — usually the most consistent sub windows of the game. In 2026-27 data, 99% of quarters have both, and the first comes before the 6:00 mark 97% of the time.

Phases

Only about a quarter of mid-quarter subs happen at TO1/TO2 themselves — many come at free throws and other dead balls. So every quarter is split into six phases and every sub lands in exactly one:

Break (lineup change before the quarter; halftime before 3Q) → Pre TO1 → TO1 → TO1–TO2 → TO2 → Post TO2. In 4Q everything after TO1 is grouped.

Rates

A rate is the share of the player's games (with that team, that season) where they checked in ▲ or out ▼ during that phase. Subs within one stoppage are netted: if A comes in for B and C then comes in for A, that reads as C in for B. Regular season only; overtime excluded.

Data

ESPN play-by-play via the sportsdataverse daily releases. Current season plus the prior five.
