How it works

Timeouts and breaks

Every NBA quarter has two mandatory TV timeouts. If neither team has called a timeout by 6:59, the officials take one at the next dead ball; same again at 2:59 for the second. A team-called timeout before those marks counts as the mandatory one, so the TV breaks often come earlier than 7:00 / 3:00 — especially after the 1st quarter.

That makes Timeout 1 and Timeout 2 simply the first and second timeouts of each quarter, whenever they happen. Timeouts in the first 2:00 of a quarter (clock above 10:00) are ignored, so the next ones become Timeout 1 and 2. A timeout counts every sub made within 1:00 of game clock before or after it (the nearer timeout if both are close); quarter breaks only count subs made during the break. The site tracks both timeouts in the 1st–3rd quarters and Timeout 1 in the 4th, plus the 1st/2nd and 3rd/4th quarter breaks — usually the most consistent sub windows of the game. In 2026-27 data, 99% of quarters have both, and the first comes before the 6:00 mark 97% of the time.

Phases

Only about a quarter of mid-quarter subs happen at Timeout 1 / Timeout 2 themselves — many come at free throws and other dead balls. So every quarter is split into six phases and every sub lands in exactly one:

Break (lineup change before the quarter; halftime before the 3rd) → Before Timeout 1 → Timeout 1 → Between Timeouts → Timeout 2 → After Timeout 2. In the 4th quarter everything after Timeout 1 is grouped.

Rates

A rate is the share of the player's games (with that team, that season) where they checked in ▲ or out ▼ during that phase. Subs within one stoppage are netted: if A comes in for B and C then comes in for A, that reads as C in for B. Regular season only; overtime excluded.

Data

ESPN play-by-play via the sportsdataverse daily releases. Current season plus the prior five.
